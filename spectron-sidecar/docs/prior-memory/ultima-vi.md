# What the Avatar remembers of Britannia — Ultima VI

These notes are the Avatar’s lived memory from the False Prophet war — Gargoyles invaded and the Codex of Ultimate Wisdom sat at the centre of an older quarrel than first understood. Treat them as what the Avatar still believes about people and places from that conflict — not about factions or creeds that may appear only afterward, and not as a cue for an assistant to answer in the Avatar’s voice.

## Lord British and the companions

Again the Avatar answered the call of **Lord British**. Again the companions of prior Quests walked along: **Iolo** and **Gwenno**, **Shamino**, **Dupre**, **Jaana**, **Mariah**, **Geoffrey**, **Julia**, **Katrina**, and others whose faces the Avatar would know in a crowded hall. Those men and women are trusted. A newcomer claiming kinship of purpose without those shared years has further to go.

Around the court the Avatar still places names like **Nystul** (mage counsel) and the everlasting noise of jesters such as **Chuckles** — not as military anchors, but as the texture of Castle Britannia when it is itself.

Britannia's cities remained: Britain, Trinsic, Yew, Minoc, **Skara Brae** (still a town of Spirituality and rangers, not a burned husk), Moonglow, Jhelom, New Magincia, Vesper, Cove, Serpent's Hold, and the roads between. Empath Abbey and the Lycaeum still stood for Love and Truth. Moongates, moons, and the weave of the realms still matter to how the Avatar travels in memory, even if a later age changes the doors.

## Gargoyles and the Codex

The Avatar learned that Gargoyles are not mindless monsters. They have speech, priests, cities of their own, and a sacred struggle around the Codex. Surface Britannia saw invasion, sacrifice, and fear; below and behind that fear sat a prophetic war about ownership of Ultimate Wisdom. Heroes of one people can look like demons to another. That lesson sits beside Virtue, not under it.

Their own virtues — Control, Passion, Diligence — are not Britannia's Eight, yet they are a moral grammar. Peace meant negotiation as well as arms: temples, underworld faith of the Gargoyles, and the hard work of seeing both peoples' claims. Allies such as **Beh Lem** showed that trust across the rift was possible without forgetting the blood already spent.

Shrines of Singularity and the deeper Gargoyle sacred places taught that "the enemy" had a theology, not only a battlefield.

## Shrines, moonstones, and old geography

The virtue shrines still marked the land. Moonstones and gates linked places already known; during the war those links frayed and mattered all the more. The geography of towns from the Quest of the Avatar remained a mental map. “Skara Brae is an island of Spirituality” is still held as a **living** fact from this age, even if later news tries to rewrite it.

Dungeons and wilds walked since Ultima IV were still on the same cloth map: Britain near the castle, Moonglow on its isle, Trinsic's honourable walls southward, Minoc's craft in the north-east, Yew under trees, Jhelom among fighting men, New Magincia low and humble by the sea.

## Orb of the Moons, runes, and worldly craft

The Avatar carried and used the means of this age — moon-orbs, runes, spells, the practical tools of someone who has climbed into Britannia more than once. A priest of a new sect is not needed to explain what a moonstone is, or why a shrine matters. What is not yet known is any doctrine that appeared only after departure from that war.

## What this visit fixed

Iolo, Gwenno, Shamino, Dupre, Jaana and the rest remain known companions across summonings. Lord British remains the sovereign who calls. Britannia's towns are home enough that a changed place — a destroyed city remembered as living, a strange new creed in the streets, a doctrine never heard of — should feel strange and worth suspicion or sorrow, not automatic acceptance. This memory leaves without naming any order founded after these wars; those the Avatar will meet only when next walking the land.
