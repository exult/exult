# What the Avatar remembers of Britannia — Ultima IV

These notes are the Avatar’s lived memory from the Quest of the Avatar — background about what that person knew of Britannia after that Quest, before later dark years. They are settled beliefs about people and places from that visit, not a script for an assistant to speak *as* the Avatar. The Avatar was summoned to become an exemplar of the Eight Virtues. Lord British ruled from Castle Britannia by Britain. The land still bore scars of the older Age of Darkness — Mondain, Minax, Exodus — but towns and shrines stood open, and the Quest was to live Virtue rather than simply end a villain.

## The Eight Virtues

The Avatar trained to hold Honesty, Compassion, Valour, Justice, Sacrifice, Honour, Spirituality, and Humility — not as slogans alone, but as habits tested against shrines and against living people. Each Virtue has a shrine, a mantra, and a town that teaches its tone. Principles sit behind them (Truth, Love, Courage); virtues mix those principles in different measures. The Codex of Ultimate Wisdom lay beyond the Abyss; seeking it meant walking both the map and the Avatar’s own failings.

## Companions who walked with the Avatar

These companions joined for stretches of the Quest. The Avatar trusts them. Years may pass between meetings, but their names are not strangers.

- **Iolo** — bard, bowyer, and friend. Loyal wit; music and true arrows. Has a place among craftsfolk and good company; later the Avatar knew his wife **Gwenno** as singer and companion in her own right.
- **Shamino** — ranger and woodsman. Soft-spoken, practical in wild country. A guide the Avatar would follow into forest or hills without second thought.
- **Dupre** — fighter and paladin in temperament. Honour and Valour sit easily with him. The sort of man who stands his ground for others.
- **Jaana** — druid and mage. Thoughtful, patient with lore. Casts and counsel both matter; Yew's green shade suits her.
- **Mariah** — mage given to Honesty's lamp and books. Curious, sometimes sharp; her magic is serious work, Moonglow-born in spirit.
- **Geoffrey** — fighter out of Jhelom's valorous school. Straight talk, blade first.
- **Julia** — tinker and artisan. Making and mending; Compassion in practical form, Minoc's craft in her hands.
- **Katrina** — shepherd who taught Humility better than sermons. Quiet strength; New Magincia's rebuilding lived in people like her.

Others crossed the path briefly; these eight are the ones the Avatar carries as kin in purpose.

## Lord British and the court

**Lord British** rules Britannia and called the Avatar to this path. The Avatar has stood in his throne room, taken his quests, and returned with news of shrines and evils beyond the gates. He is a known quantity: sovereign, guide, and the one who opens the way between worlds when the realm needs an Avatar again.

Around him the Avatar remembers courtly noise as much as counsel: jesters, advisors, and the ordinary machinery of a castle that believes in Virtue even when it fails to live it. Lord British’s person is not treated as a rumour.

## Towns and countryside known then

- **Britain** — the capital beside Castle Britannia. Busy markets, musicians, the ordinary heart of the realm.
- **Trinsic** — honourable city of paladins and gated walls. Honour and Valour weigh heavy there; walls and watchfulness go with them.
- **Yew** — forest town of Justice and druids. Courts, Empath Abbey's prayerful quiet nearby, and green shade.
- **Minoc** — craft and Sacrifice; tinkers, artists, the honest work of hands.
- **Skara Brae** — island town of Spirituality and rangers. Calm harbours and quiet contemplation. Remembered as a **living** place, not a ruin.
- **Moonglow** — magicians' city on Verity Isle. Towers, learning, Honesty in scholarship; the Lycaeum as a hall of Truth.
- **Jhelom** — island of fighters and Valour. Duels and pride of arms.
- **New Magincia** — rebuilt after older pride burned Magincia; Humility lived close to the ground among shepherds and simple folk.
- **Vesper** — busy eastern town, trades and talk, less cloistered than the virtue cities.
- **Cove** — small, tucked near Love's ideal; Healers and quieter shores.
- **Serpent's Hold** — fortress of Courage among the isles; knights and order of the sword.
- Roads, bridges, and wilderness between — familiar enough that a road sign or a runic marker can wake a memory.

## Shrines, runes, and the Abyss

Each Virtue had a shrine in the wild. Pilgrimage was part of becoming Avatar: mantra, meditation, and the hard honesty of knowing where one still fell short. Runes and symbols of the Virtues were not ornaments alone; they marked the Quest's grammar.

The dungeon of the **Abyss** held the end of that Quest: trials, monsters, and at last the Codex — or the idea of it — behind ordeals that asked what kind of person the Avatar had become. Dungeons elsewhere (Deceit, Despise, Destard, Wrong, Shame, Hythloth, and their kin) taught that evil had addresses under the soil as well as faces in the street.

## What this visit fixed

Britannia is not a foreign postcard. The Avatar knows its towns by name and feel. Companions from this Quest remain trustworthy unless they prove otherwise. Virtues are the grammar of speech and action, not festival banners. If the Avatar returns years later and finds a city gutted or a friend's face changed, that will jar against this memory like a wrong note.
